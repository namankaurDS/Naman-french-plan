3-Month Learning Phases

Each phase builds on the last — sentence patterns first, then conversation, then fluency.

Phase 1
Foundation
15 Mar → 15 Apr · A1 → A2
Build complete sentence patterns. Greetings, introductions, daily routines, simple admin phrases. Learn everything as full sentences — "Je dois envoyer ce document", "Pouvez-vous m'aider ?" Never isolated words.
A1 → A2
Phase 2
Conversation Bridge
15 Apr → 15 May · A2 → B1
Real dialogue practice. Phone calls, meetings, emails, expressing opinions, past & future tenses. Begin shadowing native speakers sentence by sentence. Focus on listening at natural speed.
A2 → B1
Phase 3
Fluency Push
15 May → 15 Jun · B1 → B2
Sustain longer conversations. Complex admin, formal emails, handling misunderstandings. Watch storytelling channels without subtitles. Write 5-sentence diary entries daily in French.
B1 → B2
⚡ Important: Since speaking & listening feel harder — in Phases 2 and 3, move 10 min from reading into extra shadowing practice. Reading is already your strong skill, so use it to push the harder skills forward.
🎯 Sentence-first rule: Never write down a single word. Always write the full sentence it came from. Your brain learns French through patterns, not vocabulary lists.

Daily 2-Hour Schedule

30 minutes per skill — same order every day. Consistency beats intensity.

👂
Block 1 — Listening
30 min · Fresh ears, first thing
Watch 1 video with French subtitles. Pause every 2–3 sentences and repeat them aloud. Focus on rhythm and how words blend together in natural French speech.
Phase 1: subtitles ON · Phase 2: French only · Phase 3: no subtitles
🗣️
Block 2 — Speaking
30 min · Shadow + practice scenarios
Shadow 5–10 sentences from your listening video. Then practice 1 scenario aloud: ordering, calling, explaining a task at work. Record yourself once a week.
Use the same video from Block 1 — the context is still fresh in your mind
📖
Block 3 — Reading
30 min · Your strongest skill
Read transcripts, dialogues, or short stories from your channels. Read aloud slowly. Note 3 complete sentences you want to reuse — not words, always full sentences.
Tip: Reading video transcripts reinforces everything from Blocks 1 & 2
✍️
Block 4 — Writing
30 min · Final block
Write 5–8 sentences using patterns from today. Start with "Aujourd'hui..." and describe a task, event, or admin situation. Phase 3: write a full professional email in French.
Daily starters: "Aujourd'hui j'ai... / Je dois... / J'aimerais..."
📅 Weekly rhythm suggestion: Mon–Fri follow the plan above. Saturday: rewatch one video from the week without subtitles. Sunday: record yourself speaking 5 sentences about your week. Keep the recordings — the progress from Week 1 to Week 12 will motivate you enormously.

Professional Tips

Techniques that make the difference between slow progress and real fluency gains in 3 months.

1
One video, four skills
Pick ONE storytelling video per day and use it across all four blocks — listen to it, shadow sentences from it, read its transcript, then write your own version of the story in 5 sentences. Maximum learning from minimum content.
2
Sentence mining, never word lists
When you find a useful phrase — "Je dois envoyer ce document avant vendredi" — copy the entire sentence. Learn it, use it, adapt it. Never break it into parts. Full sentences build real fluency because that is how French actually works in your brain.
3
Shadow for speaking confidence
After listening, immediately pause and repeat the sentence with the same rhythm, speed and intonation as the native speaker. This is how you fix pronunciation and build speaking confidence faster than any grammar study. 5 sentences per day is enough — do it every day.
4
Weekly professional scenario focus
Every week, pick one specific work scenario — scheduling a meeting, sending a request email, explaining a delay, greeting a new colleague — and make that week's writing and speaking practice entirely about that one scenario. By June you'll have 12 scenarios mastered.
5
Record yourself every Sunday
On Sundays, record yourself speaking 5 sentences about your week in French. Keep every recording. By June, the gap between Week 1 and Week 12 will be dramatic and very motivating — this is your best progress measurement tool.
6
Fill the listening gap with passive exposure
Since listening is your harder skill, add 10–15 minutes of Easy French or InnerFrench as background audio during meals or commuting. Passive exposure to natural French rhythm compounds enormously over 3 months — even without active concentration.
7
Phase 3 stretch — B2 test moment
In June, watch one full Easy French video with NO subtitles and write down everything you understood. Then rewatch with subtitles and compare. This is your B2 benchmark. The gap should be small by then — and seeing that will be one of the most satisfying moments of the whole 3 months.
8
Admin French — 5 key sentences to master first
"Pourriez-vous m'envoyer... ?" · "Je vous contacte au sujet de..." · "Veuillez trouver ci-joint..." · "Je confirme notre rendez-vous..." · "Merci de me tenir informé(e)." — These 5 sentence structures cover 80% of everyday professional French communication. Learn them in Week 1.
